Through the hollow halls of midnight, winds arose with voices sighing,
Round the tower lost in sorrow, where the restless tides return.
Here I stand in doleful grieving, heart unhealing, ne’er reprieving,
By the cliffs where ghosts are weaving shadows dark and cold and stern—
Took mine own, my house lay sundered, left me thus with heart that burned—
Gone in silence, now to mourn.

Drear the waters, drear the tower, moaned the sea at midnight’s hour,
Cold the stone where Daura weepeth, lost in tides that twist and churn.
Once she laughed like light of morning, bright as dawn my house adorning,
Now she calleth through the mourning, yet her voice shall ne’er return—
Winds that whisper, waves that wander, chant a dirge I ne’er shall learn—
Chant forever, now to mourn.

Daura, fairest, ever blameless, lost to fate so grim and nameless,
Still I see thee, arms extended, crying o’er the tide forlorn.
Armar vowed to love thee solely, ne’er to leave thee lost and lowly,
Ne’er he saw the warning growing, ne’er he knew what lay unborn—
Ne’er he dreamt deceit stood waiting, ne’er he knew what fate had borne—
Doom unyielding, now to mourn.

Erath, traitor, dark deceiver, wove his words like chains that sever,
Spoke his lies with whispers hollow, lured mine own from yonder shore.
Took her forth through waves unguarded, led her where the seas lay parted,
Left her weeping, lone, discarded, winds that stole her cries once more—
Soft she lingers, faint she calleth, fading slow in breathwinds torn—
Fading ever, now to mourn.

Arindal, my son, undaunted, bold in war yet peace untainted,
Walked with honor, held with kindness, strode with pride where vows were sworn.
Saw the waves where Daura waited, heard her cries all desolated,
Took the oar and sought his sister, steered his skiff through torrents worn—
Faced the storm and crossed the waters, braved the depths where dangers warn—
Bound to save her, now to mourn.

Through the storm his bark was swaying, waves against the shore were braying,
On the sand where Erath waited, bound by neither chain nor thorn.
Arindal, with fury burning, seized the traitor, false and yearning,
Lashed him fast where boughs were twisting, to the oak of fate foresworn—
Left him wailing, left him pleading, cursed beneath the skies forlorn—
Trapped in silence, now to mourn.

Armar, blind in wrath and sorrow, saw the night forsake the morrow,
Saw mine own upon the waters, saw his love in fate foresworn.
Ne’er he knew what hand had saved her, ne’er he knew what falsehood paved it,
Drew his bow in bitter fury, loosed the shaft with vengeance worn—
Pierced my son, my strength, my shelter, fell he silent, pale, outworn—
Veiled in shadow, now to mourn.

Arindal, though wan and failing, gripped the oar as sight was paling,
Spoke no word, no curse, no whisper, though his breast with wound was torn.
Still he rowed though death stood near him, still the waves rose wild to steer him,
Still the storm, unheeding, bore him, still the tides in sorrow mourn—
Rowed in silence, rowed unyielding, till his soul from flesh was shorn—
Lost to shadow, now to mourn.

Then the storm, in fury breaking, rent the bark with torrents quaking,
Snapped the wood and cleft asunder all the hope that once was borne.
Arindal to sea was given, Daura grasped for aid—but driven,
Far beyond the reach of father, stranded lone on rock forlorn—
Lashed by winds and left to perish, faint beneath the breathless morn—
Fate unchanging, now to mourn.

Armar, raving, tempest-haunted, hurled him forth to darkness daunted,
Plunged into the tide in madness—heart betrayed and reason torn.
Sought he Daura, lost and crying? Fled he guilt or was he dying?
None can tell what soul was whisper’d when the storm in wrath was born—
Rose the wave and clasp’d upon him, left no trace for those who mourn—
Gone forever, now to mourn.

Daura, left in grief and wailing, called for me through midnight crying,
On the rock where death attended, torn by waves that mocked in scorn.
Through the night her voice grew weaker, fading as the dawn shone meeker,
Soft she pled, though none did reach her—lost betwixt the sea and morn—
Old I stood upon the shoreline, hearing, helpless, weak and worn—
Heard her silence, now to mourn.

Storms may break and seas may sever, yet my heart shall hush—oh never,
Still I stand where past endeavored, whispering of days outworn.
By the cliffs where Daura faded, by the tide where fate paraded,
By the winds that bore her sorrow—shadows drift where night winds soar—
Shadows pale, through midnight gliding, silent both, their father scorning—
Passing onward, now to mourn.
