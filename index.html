Graduate student in Computing (Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning), Imperial College London

Building on a strong foundation in mathematics and optimization from the University of Waterloo and Cornell University, my passion lies in understanding and building complex systems from first principles.

I enjoy challenges where theoretical ideas meet implementation — where algorithms transform into real, efficient systems. Across my projects I have designed and implemented systems ranging from a pipelined RISC-V CPU and a Scheme interpreter to physically based path tracers in C++, and my technical write-ups reflect a commitment to rigorous analysis and clear communication.

I am now applying this systems-level and mathematical rigor to challenges in AI, and I am seeking opportunities at the intersection of high-performance systems and machine learning.

PyTorchDiffusion Audio · 2026

CFM-SVC: Diffusion-Based Audio Processing Pipeline

Built two flow-matching singing voice conversion systems. V1 trains a Diffusion Transformer from scratch on 1024-dim DAC codec latents (~82M params); V2 adapts a frozen pretrained F5-TTS with rank-16 LoRA injected into every attention projection plus a conditioning adapter, cutting trainable parameters 89% to ~5M. Conditioning fuses Whisper PPG, HuBERT embeddings, CREPE pitch tracks, and speaker d-vectors; a two-stage protocol first trains on multi-speaker singing, then fine-tunes only the speaker projection on the target's speech clips — no singing required. Inference runs Euler ODE sampling through a frozen Vocos vocoder, with a Gradio demo and SLURM training scripts.

Cryptography · ML

Studying S-Boxes and the Feistel Network in DES with the Multilayer Perceptron Model

Conducted a linear cryptanalysis of the Data Encryption Standard (DES) using a multilayer perceptron (MLP) model trained on plaintext–ciphertext pairs, validating DES S-box semantic security and demonstrating resistance to chosen-plaintext attacks despite 100% training convergence. Implemented Python and Mathematica experiments to analyze how neural networks approximate S-box mappings and how imperfect S-box designs can be exploited; visualized how increased Feistel rounds degrade ML accuracy, showing how learning behavior reveals structural patterns in block ciphers.

University of Waterloo — supervised by Prof. Gautam Kamath. Code and implementation details available upon request.
